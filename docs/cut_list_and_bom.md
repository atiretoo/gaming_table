## 5. Step-by-Step Shellac Finishing Guide for Beginners

Shellac is one of the most forgiving, beautiful finishes in fine woodworking:

1. **Surface Prep**:
   - Sand all Baltic birch surfaces progressively with 120, 150, and 220 grit.
   - For the exposed multi-ply edges: sand up to 320 grit. The end grain of the birch plies will become glass-smooth!
   - Vacuum thoroughly and wipe with a clean tack cloth.
2. **First Coat (Sealer / Wash Coat)**:
   - Thin Zinsser SealCoat 1:1 with denatured alcohol (yielding a ~1-lb cut).
   - Apply smoothly with a pad or brush. It will soak into the wood pores and dry within 20–30 minutes.
   - Lightly scuff-sand with 320-grit paper to knock down any raised grain. Wipe clean.
3. **Body Coats (2 to 3 Coats)**:
   - Apply SealCoat straight from the can (2-lb cut) using long, smooth, overlapping strokes with a lint-free cloth pad or ox-hair brush.
   - *Key rule with shellac*: Do not over-brush or go back over a tacky wet spot — shellac dissolves into previous coats seamlessly, so catch any missed spots on the next coat!
   - Allow 45 minutes to 1 hour between coats.
   - Scuff very lightly with 400-grit sandpaper between coats to remove dust nibs.
4. **Final Rub-Out (Satin Luster)**:
   - After the final coat cures overnight, buff the surface using 0000 steel wool or a gray Scotch-Brite pad lubricated with a small dab of clear paste wax.
   - Buff off with a microfiber towel.
   - **Result**: A warm, silky-smooth satin finish that highlights the natural beauty and exposed edge laminations of the Baltic birch without plastic sheen.
